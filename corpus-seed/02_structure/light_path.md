## Pair pressure

Dæg and Cen are not generic illumination and darkness. Day is public, divine, famous, and enjoyed across unequal conditions. Torch is local, made, dangerous, and materially available as secure interior light chiefly where elites have room to rest beside fire. The chapter therefore separates universality of exposure from equality of access.

Darkness is not mere negation. It is inhabited by other perceivers and predators. Fire can illuminate, embolden, destroy, preserve through copying, and erase the archive. The screen transposes these pressures into the present: it is a human-made envoy of light carrying sense and nonsense, world and darkness, feeding while consuming.

## Contraries without collapse

*X≠Y≠Z: Contraries and Negations* states the chapter's strongest resistance to binary reduction. X and Y require one another; Z maintains rather than abolishes their tension and, like metaphor, joins sameness with difference. *Or are you both?* repeats the refusal through wave language and the question of messenger versus message. Scientific, theological, literary, and divinatory speech touch without becoming interchangeable warrants.

## Transmission and fire

The two burning instructions must remain distinct. *How to Burn it All Down* is fury-driven literary satire. *How to Burn the Cotton Library* reconstructs a historical vulnerability in the house of preservation: inherited manuscripts gathered for national custody are destroyed through material conditions of storage, architecture, fuel, fire, and sleep. Neither is neutral practical guidance. Together they make preservation and destruction inseparable risks of transmission.
