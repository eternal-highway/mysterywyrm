## Pair pressure

Gifu and Eþel are not simply generosity and belonging. Gift becomes the material and immaterial support by which the exile survives; home becomes the place where justice, propriety, assembly, and inspiration can occur. Yet the interior repeatedly breaks both securities. Family can banish. The house can be riddling. A gift cannot necessarily be touched or bought. Z remains audible after exclusion. The Wife’s speaker seeks the departed beloved but inhabits separation. The chapter therefore holds gift and home under the pressure of exile rather than resolving exile into a sentimental return.

## Gift, home, and the roofed rune

The glyph pages make the relation graphic. Gifu is a crossing of lines and a sound that changes with its neighbors. Eþel is taught by carving Gifu and placing a roof over it: gift becomes housed without ceasing to be gift. This does not establish that home possesses the gift. It shows enclosure as an additional relation, one that can shelter, identify, constrain, or exclude.

## Theological and temporal pressure

The angel page stages communication between duration-bound humans and beings described through phenomenal flux. The castings place mercy, help, right judgement, collective speech, and inspiration within a divinatory field. *Translating Eþel* then binds Home to the Mann → Lagu → Ing → Eþel tempo sequence. These are theological, divinatory, and formal claims made by the site; none should be reduced to psychological advice or independently certified cosmology.
