## What the pair does

Ger and Beorc are not simply year and tree. Ger names recurrence, but its glyph is unstable in size, sound, shape, and neighboring identities. Beorc bears without bloom or offspring, yet offers leaves, survival, beauty, and twigs by which the future is sought. The pair therefore places futurity in signs already present: seasonal change, food, memory, language, divine gift, grief, and action.

The chapter repeatedly refuses a remote linear future. Beginnings are calculation devices; the year is several changing measurements; the dead return in memory without waiting for an anniversary; divination exposes the interpreter as part of the instrument; and the site insists that the favorable time is now. This does not abolish the future. It relocates future pressure within the present.

## Theological and memorial force

The Ger stanza says that God lets the earth sprout and that the holy king of heaven gives bright fruits to rich and needy. That agency is preserved as theological speech before any agricultural, calendrical, or psychological translation.

*Y is for Year's Mind* does not offer recurrence as consolation. It places calculation beside the irreversibility of death, the violence of receiving news, the uncertainty of foreknowledge, and the continued presence of the dead in memory. The chapter's cycle therefore includes a wound that does not close merely because a year returns.
