## Observed

The page treats gift-giving as a material and reputational economy. Ornament and honor meet in *gleng*; what is given establishes worth and remembrance, especially in a world where things and lives are temporary. The stanza’s final pressure is the exile who lacks other support.

## Site-authored claims

- Gift exchange is presented as central to the social world addressed by the Rune Poem.
- Ornament, honor, memory, mercy, and sustenance are joined but not interchangeable.

## Working inference

The intangible remainder of gift—honor and memory—outlasts the object and becomes a form of earthly continuity.
