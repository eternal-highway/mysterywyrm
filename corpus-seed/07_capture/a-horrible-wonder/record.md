## Observed

The page translates the Beowulf mere as a place of nightly horror: fire on flowing water, blood and gore, unknowable depth, an animal choosing death on shore rather than entering, and water rising into storm until the sky mourns and weeps.

## Site-authored claims

- The Beowulf passage makes water a depth no living wisdom masters and joins it to fire, blood, death, storm, and mourning.

## Working inference

The page prevents the chapter's cycle from becoming sanitary. Water carries the dead, the infernal, and the unmeasured alongside weather and renewal.
