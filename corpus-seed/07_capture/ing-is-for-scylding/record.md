## Observed

The page receives the opening Scyld ship-burial from Beowulf: the beloved ruler departs to God; companions lay him in a ring-prowed vessel with weapons, armor, ornaments, and treasures from afar. His people return the giver of rings to the sea with no fewer burial goods than those once sent with him.

## Site-authored claims

- The page presents Scyld as an Ing-descended ruler whose death, divine journey, seaborne departure, and treasure-laden burial bind prosperity to gift and return.

## Working inference

These are burial goods in the strict material sense and in the project's governing sense: relations, weapons, words, gifts, lineage, body, ship, sea, and memory are what make transmission and return possible.
