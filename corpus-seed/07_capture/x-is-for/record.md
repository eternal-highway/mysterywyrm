## Observed

The page begins from X's scarcity at the beginnings of English words and its greater availability inside or at the ends of words. It connects the X-shaped Eolhx rune with the word-final X-sound of `eolhx`, then tests a large field of alternative Old English X-ending words: knives, hair, wax, fish, beasts, and birds. It closes by asking what X is for rather than settling the question.

Navigation enters from *Rune Casting: Eolhx* and exits to *Fen*.

## Site-authored claims

- Old English has no word-initial X and few word-final X words.
- The rune's X value created a compositional problem for the Rune Poem poet.
- Eolhx was a purposeful solution within a Latin-influenced linguistic environment.
- Other X-ending Old English words could have served and disclose several alternate semantic fields.
- X remains underused and difficult to assign in alphabet books.

## Working inference

The page refuses a single symbolic gloss for X. It produces a surplus of possible titles and then leaves the title incomplete: *X is for*. The missing complement is itself active, but should remain an observed grammatical opening rather than a solved riddle.
