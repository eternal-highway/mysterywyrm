## Observed

The chapter page holds together fate's changeability and certainty. Omens may help people respond to events, but death remains unavoidable. Gods guide and decide without possessing absolute immunity from fate; human discernment and action remain real inside a changing, interconnected world.

## Site-authored claims

- Wyrd is described as a process of changing circumstances and coming-into-being, both greater than and involving deities.
- Human beings retain discernment and responsibility for responding even when events lie beyond their control.
- Death is the cessation of change and the unavoidable face of fate.

## Working inference

The chapter refuses both total control and passive determinism. Fate is transmitted as a field of divine, worldly, temporal, and human agencies whose asymmetry makes response necessary.
