# Entry Record: The Middle

Source: https://lettersfortitles.com/the-middle/

## Site-authored orientation

The work instructs a new reader that the encountered page is neither a conventional beginning nor merely a latest post. The present is the middle: the point of contact between past and future.

The site then states its central structural discovery:

- the 29 stanzas of the Old English Rune Poem are arranged from outside toward a single middle;
- the first and last stanzas form a thematic pair, then the second and penultimate, continuing inward;
- stanza 15, Eolhx, stands at the turn;
- the 29-rune sequence can be read as a lunar calendar: increase toward the middle, then decrease by reflection;
- it can also be traversed as a unicursal labyrinth: enter, turn in the middle, and return by a self-similar but non-identical path;
- the resulting book is to do what the poem does, not merely report upon it.

## Consequence for the corpus

The corpus must retain at least four simultaneous orders:

1. **Publication order** — the work written forward in time.
2. **Retrospective link order** — later pages reaching backward and altering the visible whole.
3. **Rune order** — Feoh through Ear.
4. **Reflected chapter order** — Feoh/Ear inward to Eolhx and outward again.

No one of these orders may silently replace the others.

## First restraint

The structural pattern is not the whole transmission. It is the first architecture that lets us carry the materials without flattening them.
