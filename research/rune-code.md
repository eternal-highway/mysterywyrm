# The Rune Code plates

Seventeen posts carry the tag `Rune Code`; twenty-five carry `Code`. Nineteen
of the corpus's twenty wordless posts are in this set. They have no text at
all — only pictures — which is why a text-first reading of the site passes
straight over them.

Each of these posts carries two images. One is the numbered character that
belongs to the sentence spelled across the filenames (see
[`cipher.md`](cipher.md)). The other is named for the post itself, and is a
photograph: usually a page of a lined notebook, sometimes objects arranged on
a table. Those are the plates.

## The system

The plates are **branch runes** — *kvistrúnar*, the Norse twig cipher. A rune
is written as a stem with twigs to either side: the count on one side gives
which *ætt* (group of the futhorc) the rune belongs to, the count on the other
gives its position inside that ætt.

The key is written out on the facing notebook page in the *Arrows* plate,
legible at the edge of the photograph: `T 3.1`, `O 1.4`, `A 4.1`. Those are
exact for the 29-rune Anglo-Saxon futhorc divided 8 / 8 / 8 / 5:

| ætt | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | F (Feoh) | U (Ur) | Þ (Thorn) | **O (Os)** | R (Rad) | C (Cen) | G (Gifu) | W (Wyn) |
| **2** | H (Hægl) | N (Nyd) | I (Is) | J (Ger) | EO (Eoh) | P (Peorþ) | X (Eolhx) | S (Sigel) |
| **3** | **T (Tiw)** | B (Beorc) | E (Eh) | M (Mann) | L (Lagu) | NG (Ing) | Œ (Eþel) | D (Dæg) |
| **4** | **A (Ac)** | Æ (Æsc) | Y (Yr) | IO (Ior) | EA (Ear) | | | |

T is the first rune of the third ætt; O the fourth of the first; A the first
of the fourth. The key and the poem's own rune order agree exactly, so the
cipher is built on the same 29 runes the whole project is built on.

## The costumes

The cipher is redrawn from scratch for every plate. The stem-and-twig skeleton
stays; the picture on top of it changes to suit the chapter:

| plate | chapter | drawn as |
|---|---|---|
| Arrows | They'll Cut You | fletched arrows, barbs along the shaft |
| Shh | Axis Mundi | one tree, curled twigs on each branch |
| The Way | By Land and By Sea | a forest of small fir trees |
| Present | You Have Nothing Else | wrapped gifts, loops of ribbon on each bow |
| You Knew it Beforehand | Fate | faces with hair to either side, then skulls |
| It Never Deceives | Trust | real leaves, laid out on a dark ground |

The *Present* plate belongs to the chapter of Gifu, the gift rune, and is drawn
as gifts. *Shh* belongs to Axis Mundi and is drawn as the world tree. The
costume is never arbitrary.

## What the plates cite

The plates are working pages, and they carry their apparatus in shot:

- **Source citations in the header.** The *Arrows* plate is headed `Par 17.27`
  — on the face of it Dante, *Paradiso* XVII.27, which is the line about the
  arrow foreseen arriving more slowly. The post is titled "Arrows" and tagged
  `Dante Alighieri`, `Paradiso`, `Bow`. Three other plates are tagged to
  *Paradiso* as well.
- **The source book, physically present.** Under the *The Way* plate lies a
  green Tenniel *Alice* — "Which way? Which way?" — flagged with numbered
  sticky tabs, though the post's own tags name the *Tao Te Ching*.
- **Notebook page numbers**, in the top corner: 53 (*The Way*), 65 (*Shh*),
  89 (*Present*). The plates are pages of one continuous notebook, and the
  numbering is a further index nobody has laid out.

## What is not yet decoded

The system is identified and the key is confirmed, but **no plate has been read
yet**. Counting twigs is the whole problem. They are hand-drawn and slanted, so
they overlap when the image is projected onto its columns, and an automatic
count cannot separate a twig from its neighbour; and only about a quarter of
the plates are drawn as arrows at all — a tree, a face and a scatter of leaves
each need their own reading. This is the next piece of work, and it is a
manual one, plate by plate.

The 300px copies in `archive/thumbs/` are too small to count from. The
full-resolution plates, which run 1080–2560px on the long edge, come down with:

```sh
python3 tools/archive.py --tag "Rune Code" --variant full \
        --images-dir archive/code --manifest data/media-code.json --skip-pages
```

That is 31 images and 79 MB — the 17 plates plus the numbered characters from
the same posts. Like the rest of the full-resolution set they are deliberately
not in git; the manifest carries their checksums.
