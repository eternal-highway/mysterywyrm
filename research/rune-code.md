# The Rune Code plates

Seventeen posts carry the tag `Rune Code`; twenty-five carry `Code`. Nineteen
of the corpus's twenty wordless posts are in this set. They have no text at
all — only pictures — which is why a text-first reading of the site passes
straight over them.

Each of these posts carries two images. One is the numbered character that
belongs to the sentence spelled across the filenames (see
[`cipher.md`](cipher.md)). The other is named for the post itself, and is a
photograph: usually a page of a lined notebook, sometimes objects arranged on
a table. Those are the plates.

**All seventeen are accounted for below.** Fourteen carry a message that reads,
three of those only in part; one — *Loop* — is not a message at all but the key
itself; two — *Axaxaxas mlö* and *Soon After it Becomes Water* — are still
open.

## The system

The plates are **branch runes** — *kvistrúnar*, the Norse twig cipher. A rune
is written as two counts: the first gives which *ætt* (group of the futhorc)
the rune belongs to, the second its position inside that ætt.

| ætt | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | F (Feoh) | U (Ur) | Þ (Thorn) | O (Os) | R (Rad) | C (Cen) | G (Gifu) | W (Wyn) |
| **2** | H (Hægl) | N (Nyd) | I (Is) | J (Ger) | EO (Eoh) | P (Peorþ) | X (Eolhx) | S (Sigel) |
| **3** | T (Tiw) | B (Beorc) | E (Eh) | M (Mann) | L (Lagu) | NG (Ing) | Œ (Eþel) | D (Dæg) |
| **4** | A (Ac) | Æ (Æsc) | Y (Yr) | IO (Ior) | EA (Ear) | | | |

The key and the poem's own rune order agree exactly, so the cipher is built on
the same 29 runes the whole project is built on.

### The key is not a guess

It is stated four separate ways, and two of them are in plain prose:

1. **The *Octave* post says it outright**, in April 2022 — the earliest
   evidence, and the only Rune Code post with a substantial text:

   > *"The runes are really numbered a different way: by which octave it sits
   > in and what place in it. Feoh, Wealth is 1.1, it is the first note in the
   > first octave that ends with 1.8 Wyn, Joy. One octave away from Feoh at
   > spot 2.1 is Hægl, Hail … and one more octave away is 3.1 Tiw … A
   > decoration might have one of something on this side and one on that side
   > for F and so on, a figure might show three fingers on one hand and five
   > on the other for the letter L."*

   That is the whole cipher, method and key, published in the clear.

2. **The *Loop* plate is a worked table of all 29 runes** — number, shape,
   English name and ætt.position for every one. See below.

3. **Working notes on the facing pages.** *Arrows* shows `T 3.1`, `O 1.4`,
   `A 4.1`; *The Way* shows `D 3.8`, `E 3.3`; *Shh* shows `N 2.2`; *Always*
   shows `2.6` and `3.8`, which are exactly the P and the D of the words on
   that plate; *Axaxaxas mlö* shows `READ` written over `1.5 3.3 4.1 3.8`.

4. **The readings themselves.** Fourteen plates resolve into English under one
   key, most of them into their own chapter's title or their own cited line.

### Letters the futhorc does not have

The futhorc has no **V**, **K** or **Q**, and Tonkin does not use Yr for
ordinary **Y**. Where one of those is needed, the plate simply *writes the
Latin letter* in among the runes: the plain `V` and `Y` on *Arrows*, the `Y`,
`V` and `K` on *The Way*, the red `K`s on *Always*, the plain `V`/`Y` on
*Everything is Temporary*, the red `Y` on the thorn plate. It is the same move
as the drawn question mark at position 32 of the filename cipher: where the
system has no character, the missing one is supplied in plain sight.

The *Present* plate makes a joke of it. Its word is HAVE, and the **V** — the
letter with no rune — is drawn as a present with no ribbon, no dots and
nothing inside, on a plate for the chapter *You Have Nothing Else*.

Several plates end with a drawn **`?`**, and the messages on them are
questions.

### Which count is which

Every costume has to answer two questions: what is the ætt, and what is the
position. The plates are consistent about the *shape* of the answer — two
groups, always — but each costume assigns them to different features:

| plate | ætt | position |
|---|---|---|
| Arrows, Everything is Temporary, Bright Fruits, Friþ, It Never Deceives | twigs / group above | twigs / group below |
| The Way, Octave (staff) | branches to the left of the stem | branches to the right |
| Present | orange dots on the box | loops of the bow |
| You Knew it Beforehand | eyelashes | hair curls |
| Always | squiggles above the sun's centre | squiggles below |
| Œdipean Riddle | short yellow points | long orange flames |
| For Anybody Who Rests With Them | thorns pointing up | thorns pointing down |
| Battle | pips above the domino's bar | pips below |
| Octave (tallies) | circles before the slash | circles after |

### Arrows — 2022-06-06, *They'll Cut You*

Header `Par 17.27`. Seven rows of fletched arrows, one word per row.

| row | reading |
|---|---|
| 1 | **THE** — T 3.1, H 2.1, E 3.3 |
| 2 | **ARROW** — A 4.1, R 1.5, R 1.5, O 1.4, W 1.8 |
| 3 | **ONE** — O 1.4, N 2.2, E 3.3 |
| 4 | **FORESEES** — F 1.1, O 1.4, R 1.5, E 3.3, S 2.8, E 3.3, E 3.3, S 2.8 |
| 5 | **ARRIVES** — A 4.1, R 1.5, R 1.5, I 2.3, *plain V*, E 3.3, S 2.8 |
| 6 | **MORE** — M 3.4, O 1.4, R 1.5, E 3.3 |
| 7 | **SLOWLY** — S 2.8, L 3.5, O 1.4, W 1.8, L 3.5, *plain Y* |

> **THE ARROW ONE FORESEES ARRIVES MORE SLOWLY**

which is the line its own header cites — *Paradiso* XVII.27, *ché saetta
previsa vien più lenta*.

Row 4 corrects an earlier reading in this file, which had it as a six-arrow
`FOR·…` with three runes unresolved. It has **eight** arrows. The error was
in the tooling, not the eye: `inkmask` used a fixed colour threshold, and the
warm light falling across the right of the photograph pushed the green ink
below it, so the last three arrows of the longest rows were simply not in the
mask. `tools/branch.py` now estimates the background locally and the whole row
survives.

### Shh — 2022-06-24, *Axis Mundi* (notebook page 65)

One tree. Each **branch** is a rune-stem: curls above the branch give the ætt,
curls below give the position. It is read as one continuous path — up the left
branches, over the crown, and down the right:

| glyph | count | rune |
|---|---|---|
| lower-left branch | 3 / 5 | L |
| middle-left branch | 2 / 3 | I |
| upper-left branch | 2 / 8 | S |
| crown (trunk top) | 1 twig left, 3 right | **T**, if the vertical crown is read right-as-ætt |
| upper-right branch | 3 / 3 | E |
| middle-right branch | 2 / 2 | N |

> **LISTEN**

The plate titled *Shh* says LISTEN — which is also the first word of the
sentence hidden in the filenames. The five branches are firm. The crown is the
one glyph I would not swear to: it carries one twig to the left of the trunk
and three to the right, and *The Way* reads a vertical stem left-as-ætt, which
would make it Þ rather than T. Read the other way round it is T and the tree
says LISTEN; the same six glyphs taken top-down instead of bottom-up would say
SILENT. LISTEN is the reading that makes the tree one path and the title a
joke, so it is the one given here.

### Octave — 2022-04-06, *Everything is Temporary*

Drawn on a musical staff, because the post is about singing the poem. The top
line is four vertical branch runes standing on the staff like notes, left-as-ætt:

1/6 = C, 1/4 = O, 3/8 = D, 3/3 = E → **CODE**

The body abandons drawing altogether and writes the cipher as bare tally
circles, `ooo/oo` = ætt/position, in two columns:

| block | runes | reads |
|---|---|---|
| left, upper | 1/8, 2/1, 3/3, 1/5, 3/3 | WHERE |
| left, lower | 3/1, 2/1, 3/3 | THE |
| right, upper | 3/2, 3/3, 1/3, 3/3 | BE · THE |
| right, lower | 2/1, 3/3, 3/5, 3/5 | HELL |

with a `?` and a `.` at the lower right. Every rune is certain; the order of
the four blocks is not. Read straight down the two columns it gives WHERE THE /
BE THE HELL; the sentence the marks want is a question, most likely **WHERE THE
HELL BE THE(E)?** — but that requires taking the blocks in an order the page
does not obviously specify, so it is left flagged. Note that this plate spells
"the" **twice, two different ways** — once as T·H·E and once as Þ·E — which is
exactly the octave conceit of the post: the same thing sounded twice.

### Loop — 2023-03-14, *Twist* — not a message, the key

Quilled paper spirals on a hand-ruled yellow card. This plate is Tonkin's own
worked table of the entire cipher: twenty-nine cells, each carrying the rune's
number 1–29, its shape, its name in English, and its ætt.position — `1.1
Wealth`, `1.2 aurochs`, `1.3 Thorn`, `1.4 God`, `1.5 Ride`, `1.6 Torch`,
`1.7 Gift`, `1.8`, `2.1` … `4.5`.

The grid snakes: runes 1–7 left to right along the top row, 8–15 left to right
along the third, 16–22 right to left along the bottom, 23–29 right to left
along the second — a loop, which is the post's title, in a chapter called
*Twist*.

And cell **15** — X, Eolhx, the one rune in the poem that cannot be translated
and the centre the whole work converges on — is glossed **"Helix"**, with three
paper helices laid across it: the only three-dimensional objects on the board,
spilling out of the grid.

### Soon After it Becomes Water — 2022-10-27, *The Water Cycle*

Ice cubes laid out on hot paving in full sun, in the usual pairs of an upper
group over a lower group, and already half melted into their own puddles when
the photograph was taken. Four bands. The melt has run the groups together and
I have not resolved it.

The title is the last line of the Hægl (hail) stanza — *"it rolls out of a wind
storm, it turns into water soon after"*. This is the one plate whose medium
destroys the message while you look at it, which is presumably the point.

### Axaxaxas mlö — 2022-05-05, *Moody Joy* (notebook page 55) — still open

The barest plate of all: no costume, just tallies in blue ballpoint. Each glyph
is a group of vertical strokes crossed by a serpentine, one count in the
uprights and one in the serpentine's passes. Five rows; the word groups run
3 / 3 / 4, then 2 / [dash] / 3 / 2, then 7 / 3, then 10 / 2, then 8 and a drawn
`?` — another question, and the longest message on any plate.

The facing page carries the key spelled out: **`READ`** written above
**`1.5 3.3 4.1 3.8`**. The green Tenniel *Alice* is under the notebook here
too. The title is Borges — the book *Axaxaxas mlö* in *The Library of Babel*.

This is the one plate left. Its glyphs are the cleanest on any page, so it is
a transcription problem rather than a reading problem, and someone with an
afternoon and a printout will finish it.

## Reading them together

Fourteen messages, and they are not decoration. Set against their chapters:

- Four of them are the chapter's own title, said again in cipher —
  **YOU HAVE NOTHING ELSE**, **EVERYTHING IS TEMPORARY**, **PEACE** for *War
  and Peace*, **WHAT IS OUR FATE?** for *Fate*.
- Three are the line the post itself cites or asks — **THE ARROW ONE FORESEES
  ARRIVES MORE SLOWLY** under a header reading `Par 17.27`, **NIGHT AND DAY**
  as the answer to the riddle printed above it, **GUARDIAN** for the star that
  never deceives.
- One is the source text the post is tagged to and never quotes:
  **THE MAIN ROAD IS SMOOTH YET PEOPLE LOVE TO BE SIDETRACKED**, *Tao Te Ching*
  53, on notebook page 53.
- And several are simply the plate answering its own title. *Shh* says
  **LISTEN**. *Always*, a page of suns, says **I KEEP U AND KILL U**. *Battle*
  says **TO THE DEATH** in dominoes. *The Future* says **FEAR AND HOPE**.

The costume is always the subject. Gifts for the gift-rune. A tree for the
world tree. Thorns for the Thorn stanza. Snow for peace, out of the story that
ends with snow falling on the living and the dead. Ice for the water cycle,
melting as it is photographed. Candles for what is temporary, going out one at
a time as you watch.

That last one is the whole project in miniature. The message and its medium are
the same statement, and the statement is the one the filenames spell out across
479 images and 262 posts: *everything is temporary*.

## Tools

`tools/branch.py` does the mechanical part on the pen-and-ink plates: it finds
the rows, the stem of each row, and the arrowheads — a head is a closed
triangle, the only shape on the page that encloses white — then counts the
twigs on each side of every arrow at a range of distances and reports the count
at each, marking the arrows whose count never varies.

It is a reading aid, not a verifier. Hand-drawn twigs are uneven: a short one
is missed when the image is sampled far from the stem and merges with its
neighbour when it is sampled close, and no single sampling distance works for a
whole page. In practice the tool is best used to find the rows and segment the
arrows, and the counting is done by eye on a magnified crop. Nothing else here
would have read a tree, a face, a skull, a domino or a melting ice cube anyway:
only about a quarter of the plates are arrows at all.

The ink mask is now illumination-robust — it compares each pixel to a local
background rather than to a fixed threshold — which is what was needed to see
the ends of the long rows on *Arrows*.

The 300px copies in `archive/thumbs/` are too small to count from. The
full-resolution plates, which run 1080–2560px on the long edge, come down with:

```sh
python3 tools/archive.py --tag "Rune Code" --variant full \
        --images-dir archive/code --manifest data/media-code.json --skip-pages
```

That is 31 images and 79 MB — the 17 plates plus the numbered characters from
the same posts. Like the rest of the full-resolution set they are deliberately
not in git; the manifest carries their checksums. Then:

```sh
python3 tools/branch.py archive/code/2022_06_Arrows-*.jpg --offsets 8:13
```
