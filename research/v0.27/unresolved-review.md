addendum, not a released adjudication or an alteration of the frozen first pass.

The review supports **P161 bottom-left 2.4**, supplies a count-based **P109 skull
sequence 3.8 / 3.3 / 4.1 / 1.3**, and identifies two necessary qualifications:
**P055's last group favors 3.1**, while **P125's upper-right six pieces admit two
2.1 groups as well as one 4.2 group**. P209's traversal remains unselected.

## P161: the local counts can be narrowed; traversal cannot

The bottom-left face has **four red flames to its left and two pale points to
its right**. The crop also includes one flame from the face above; that flame
must not be assigned to the bottom-left face. The six attached projections
support **2.4** with high confidence under the pale/red class convention.
The inherited **4.3** would require seven projections and the opposite class
distribution; this crop does not support it. This is a coordinate correction,
even though Ger and Yr can both be rendered Y in the candidate convention.

The second face of the top row has two pale/yellow projections and one red/orange
projection. The middle-right face supplies a useful control: its crown likewise
contains one red/orange projection next to four yellow projections of comparable
length. Thus absolute exposed length is not a reliable class boundary within
this plate. Applying the same **color-class rule** to both faces gives **2.1**
and **4.1**, respectively. Confidence is medium-high for this classification.
The earlier 3.0 alternative remains a description of a length-only rule, but
it is a weaker rule in light of the middle-right control. No hidden ray is needed.

In the existing left-to-right spatial rows, the resulting sequence is:

| Row | Coordinates | Mechanical transliteration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3.1 2.1 1.7 2.3 2.2 | THGIN |
| 2 | 3.8 2.2 4.1 | DNA |
| 3 | 2.4 4.1 3.8 | YAD |

Reversing each row gives NIGHT / AND / DAY. The plate does not visibly mark a
start or row direction. That familiar phrase remains a traversal interpretation,
not evidence selecting its own order. Recommend retaining the carrier's unresolved
status for traversal while promoting the bottom-left correction and the
color-based top-row count as explicitly scoped findings.

## P055: three leaves above one is the better local rule

There are eight separated groups in two rows of four. The final group contains
four physical leaves: **two diagonals plus a central vertical leaf in an upper
fan**, then **one detached vertical leaf below**. Shadows and internal pale
stripes are not extra leaves.

The immediately preceding group is a four-leaf upper fan above a detached
horizontal leaf. That fan already combines diagonal and near-vertical leaves
into one count. Elsewhere, a fan of three leaves below two uprights is also
treated as one cluster. These controls favor counting **spatial clusters**, not
reclassifying individual leaves solely by their axis.

| Row | Coordinates under the upper/lower cluster rule | Mechanical result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.7 1.2 4.1 1.5 | GUAR |
| 2 | 3.8 2.3 4.1 3.1 | DIAT |

Confidence is high for the final four physical leaves and medium-high for the
3/1 cluster assignment. A **2/2** diagonal-versus-vertical split is geometrically
possible, but applies a different rule at the final group and splits its upper
fan. It should not be presented as equally well supported by the repeated layout.

The resulting literal sequence is **GUARDIAT**, without correction to a word.
This supports neither inherited complete reading. Recommend preserving the
alternative 2.2 with its weaker rule explicitly recorded, rather than claiming
GUARDIAN established. Whether the arrangement contains an intended exception,
an error, or another rule remains unknown; image-local evidence does not decide.

## P109: a supported skull derivation, with an explicit rule change

The four skulls have two distinguishable classes of appendage: outlined bones
with widened ends, and single-stroke hairs, some with multiple curls. Counting
each whole bone and each independently rooted hair strand gives:

| Skull, left to right | Whole bones | Distinct hair strands | Coordinate | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | 8 | 3.8 | D |
| 2 | 3 | 3 | 3.3 | E |
| 3 | 4 | 1 | 4.1 | A |
| 4 | 1 | 3 | 1.3 | TH |

For skull 1, the bones lie upper-right, lower-right, and lower-left. The eight
hairs comprise three at the crown, three on the left side, and two on the right.
Skull 2 has upper-left/lower-left/lower-right bones and three crown hairs. Skull
3 has four corner bones and one crown hair. Skull 4 has one lower-right bone
and three crown hairs; the several turns on its rightmost hair belong to one
strand. Counting the two outlines of each bone separately would double the bone
count and is not the whole-object rule used here. Teeth and eye sockets are not
used in this derivation.

The counts are high-confidence visual observations; **using bones in place of
lashes as the first coordinate is a medium-confidence inferred rule**, not an
explicitly labeled instruction. It preserves the earlier strand-based second
coordinate and works consistently across all four skulls without inserting,
deleting, or splitting an appendage. The four coordinates mechanically yield
**DEATH**. This resolves the formerly missing four-unit extraction under a
stated, visually supported rule; it does not prove that no alternative feature
rule was intended.

The lower smiling faces were also checked in crops. Their strand counts support
the existing CHEER / UP* / IT / IS* extraction. With the skull rule stated, the
lower field can now be recorded as **CHEER UP* IT IS* DEATH**, medium confidence
overall. This does not validate the seed's full lower-field segmentation; only
its final DEATH overlaps. Retain the existing upper-field result and both
historical readings rather than assigning the complete carrier wholesale to
either lineage. Recommend recording this as a supported new lower-field reading
with its feature-rule qualification, not four entirely unextracted skulls.

## P125: distinguish present pieces from inferred group boundaries

The first-line left field visibly contains two upper pieces and eight lower
pieces. The upper-right field contains six pieces: four upper and two lower.
Those **present-object counts** are supported with high confidence. Water and
reflections do not erase them.

However, merging all six right-field pieces into one glyph is not forced. The
left pair of upper pieces sits above one lower piece; the right pair sits above
the other lower piece. There is a horizontal gap between these local sets.
Using an x-boundary near **1570** in the source separates those two 2/1 sets;
this is a grouping proposal, not a cut drawn on the source. The surrounding
water partly joins their regions and cannot alone establish a single glyph.

| First-line grouping | Coordinates | Mechanical result |
|---|---|---|
| Left field; merged right field | 2.8 4.2 | S*AE |
| Left field; two local right-hand sets | 2.8 2.1 2.1 | S*HH |

Both preserve exactly the same observed pieces. The second is a credible
alternative with medium confidence from local alignment; it is not selected
because SHH makes sense. Conversely, the first must not be described as a
uniquely confirmed coordinate sequence simply because its pieces are countable.
The below-line LET / US* / MELT result was not in dispute here and is retained.

Recommend replacing any claim of uniquely established first-line SAE with these
two alternatives. The top line is not wholly uncountable, but its segmentation
remains uncertain. Neither alternative reproduces SO under the observed counts.
No conclusion about vanished ice, earlier layout, or elapsed melting time follows
from this one image. These two proposals do not exhaust every imaginable grouping.
